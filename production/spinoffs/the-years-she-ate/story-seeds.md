# The Years She Ate: Story Seeds

These are anthology candidates, not individually locked stories.

## The First Offering

An early voluntary lifespan surrender becomes the historical seed for later sacred offering culture.

The original event may be practical, desperate, and personal.

Centuries later people remember it as holy.

## Seven Years

A person deliberately destroys a Brulee-attuned mirror during a political conflict.

The actual Charlotte punishment removes seven years of lifespan.

The rebellion disappears from popular memory.

The superstition survives:

> **Break a mirror and lose seven years.**

## Mama's Justice

A family whose loved ones were murdered by a pirate petitions the Orchard after the Government fails to catch him.

Katakuri returns with the pirate alive.

From the family's POV, the Charlotte system is justice.

The reader understands what "harvest" means next.

## Festival of Years

A joyous public festival of food, marriages, competitions, ancestor stories, and voluntary lifespan offerings.

The surface experience is sincere celebration.

The administrative background quietly reveals that this year's donations still do not meet the dynasty's growing maintenance need.

## The Island Mama Wouldn't Abandon

An island is strategically obsolete and expensive to defend.

Every administrator recommends leaving.

Linlin refuses because the island chose her during a crisis almost two centuries earlier.

The irrational decision is both sentimental and geopolitically expensive.

## The Mirror Bride

A newly married household receives a Brulee-attuned mirror.

It is blessing, emergency infrastructure, family heirloom, and surveillance node at the same time.

## Pudding Knows

An innocent defendant begs for Pudding to read the relevant memories because no ordinary court can prove the truth.

The story makes memory invasion somebody's only hope for justice.

## The God Who Grew Hungry

A later-era Charlotte territory celebrates the same offering ritual shown in an earlier story.

The required lifespan contribution is much larger now.

Nobody at the festival knows how small the original offering once was.

## The Year Mama Almost Died

A historical crisis nearly kills Linlin or collapses the dynasty.

The real event is messy and contingent.

Later religion remembers it as proof that Mama cannot die.

## The Day After God

After Linlin's defeat, a protected territory has to decide what her absence actually means.

Freedom arrives together with raiders, succession claims, religious panic, and the question of what institutions should replace the ones people hated but relied on.
