# Brook

> **Foundational direction:** Brook is not a Rumbar Pirate. His second life is not merely transportation to Laboon; it is the central problem of his character.

## Core

> **Everyone else got to die. Brook didn't.**

Brook resurrects after losing his own pirate crew and then spends decades among the wreckage of people whose lives ended while his did not.

He does not know what to do with an accidental extra life.

So he starts giving it away to the dead.

## Brook's crew

Brook had his **own** pirate crew roughly fifty years before the main story.

They are separate from the Rumbar Pirates.

Binks' Sake remains a common pirate song of the era.

Brook's crew can still die one by one while playing it until Brook is left alone, preserving the musical death sequence without requiring him to be a Rumbar Pirate.

A classic pirate-animal relationship, including the current parrot direction, can deepen why Brook later understands the Rumbar crew's promise to Laboon. The exact animal material is implementation, not foundation.

## The graveyard years

After resurrection, Brook eventually reaches the Florian Triangle / graveyard of failed ships.

He boards wrecks.

He reads logbooks.

He learns names.

He talks to skeletons as though crews are still present.

He finds promises nobody can finish and begins finishing them himself.

At first this gives his second life purpose.

Over decades it becomes unhealthy.

He accumulates dead crews inside his routine until the line between:

> **what they would say**

and

> **what they are saying to me**

starts to blur.

## The Rumbar Pirates

The Rumbar Pirates remain a separate crew.

They still meet Laboon, leave him with Crocus, promise to return, enter the Grand Line, and die.

Brook later finds their wreckage/logs among the dead he has been collecting.

He adds their unfinished promise to his own list:

> **Tell Laboon what happened.**

He may ask whales he encounters:

> **Are you Laboon?**

Laboon matters because Brook understands what it means for an animal to wait for people who will never return, not because the universe made Brook secretly one of the exact pirates the Straw Hats already promised to help.

Possible eventual line:

> **Laboon... I knew the people who loved you.**

## Character movement

Brook begins with:

> **I have to finish what the dead couldn't.**

The Straw Hats force another possibility into his life:

> **I can remember the dead without giving them the rest of my life.**

Survival is allowed to create new wants.

Remembering somebody is not the same thing as becoming their unfinished life.

## What is not foundational

The following can still be changed without abandoning Peak Brook:

- exact number and type of promises he carries
- the parrot implementation
- exact wreck / logbook sequence
- exact order in which he learns about Laboon
- precise comedy / hallucination balance
- his possible connection to Charlotte Linlin
