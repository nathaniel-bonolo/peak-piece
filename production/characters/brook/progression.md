# Brook: Progression

## 1. A life that should have ended

Brook dies with his own crew and returns after the moment has already passed.

His resurrection is not victory.

Everybody else got an ending.

He got continuation.

## 2. The dead become company

Isolation makes wrecks and skeletons easier to treat as people who are still present.

Logbooks let him reconstruct voices, habits, destinations, jokes, debts, lovers, children, and unfinished plans.

## 3. Purpose through promises

Brook starts carrying unfinished promises because completing them gives his second life a use.

This is initially compassionate.

It also lets him avoid asking what **Brook** wants.

## 4. Accumulation

Decades of other people's unfinished lives become weight.

Brook can remember a dead person's preferences more easily than his own.

He may talk to people who are not there and occasionally stop knowing whether he is remembering, imagining, or answering himself.

## 5. Rumbar / Laboon

The Rumbar logbooks give him one of the clearest unfinished promises he has ever found.

Laboon becomes a high-priority promise without retroactively making Brook part of that crew.

## 6. Straw Hats

The Straw Hats do not tell Brook to forget the dead.

They keep making new memories with him anyway.

For the first time in decades, his calendar begins filling with obligations to living people and wants that are actually his.

## 7. Choice

Brook still honors promises where he can.

The change is that he stops treating every dead person's unfinished life as a commandment.

> **I can remember the dead without giving them the rest of my life.**

## Progression sentence

> **Brook begins by using his accidental second life to carry everybody who lost theirs; he grows into believing that survival permits him to want a life of his own.**
