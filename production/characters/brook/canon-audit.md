# Brook: Canon Audit

## Canon baseline

Canon Brook is a Rumbar Pirate whose crew promised Laboon they would return. Brook dies, revives through the Yomi Yomi no Mi, survives alone, and eventually joins the Straw Hats who have independently met Laboon.

The Rumbar death sequence and Binks' Sake carry enormous emotional value.

## What canon accomplishes

Canon creates a powerful delayed connection between Brook and Laboon and gives the Straw Hats a direct living bridge to people Laboon loved.

That is a real strength.

## Peak Piece's different question

Peak Piece deliberately asks:

> **What does decades of accidental survival actually do to Brook?**

The concern is not that canon's Laboon reveal has no emotion.

The concern is that Brook's resurrection and fifty years of isolation can carry a much larger character arc than mainly preserving one old promise until the Straw Hats happen to meet its surviving member.

## Peak Piece response

Brook has his own crew.

The Rumbar Pirates remain separate.

Brook lives among wrecks, learns dead people's unfinished promises, and gradually spends his second life carrying lives that ended.

His arc becomes learning that memory does not require surrendering his entire future to the dead.

## What we preserve

- Binks' Sake
- a crew dying one by one while the music continues
- Brook's absurd comedy and skeleton identity
- decades of isolation
- the Rumbar Pirates
- Laboon's waiting and the Rumbar promise
- Brook eventually becoming part of Laboon's emotional resolution

## What we intentionally change

- Brook is not Rumbar
- Laboon is not the singular reason his fifty-year survival matters
- Brook develops a second-life / dead-promises arc
- his relationship to Laboon comes through witnessing the dead rather than coincidence of membership

This direction is foundational to Peak Piece.

That does not mean every scene is locked.
