# Tashigi: Canon Audit

## Canon baseline

Canon Tashigi is a Marine swordswoman with a strong interest in named swords, a desire to remove famous blades from criminals, and recurring frustration around how others read her as a woman who resembles Kuina.

## What canon appears to be doing

Canon uses Tashigi to intersect Zoro's relationship with swords, Kuina, gender, and Marine morality while also giving Smoker a partner with her own sense of justice.

Unless a source explicitly confirms authorial intent, this is interpretation rather than a claim about Oda's private intention.

## Peak Piece response

Peak keeps the sword obsession and Marine role but refuses to make proving herself as a woman swordsman her defining internal problem.

She already believes in her own strength.

Her stronger axis is weapons: provenance, craftsmanship, custody, collection, misuse, and the difference between preserving a blade and possessing it.

## Why this solution

The change gives Tashigi a more specific identity that can generate conflict without requiring sexist dismissal to remain the engine of her scenes.

It also strengthens her connection to Zoro through an actual disagreement about swords rather than making him the benchmark she needs to beat for self-worth.

## What we preserve

- Marine identity
- sword knowledge
- desire to recover famous blades from criminals
- partnership with Smoker
- recurring intersection with Zoro

## What we intentionally change

Peak does not center Tashigi on proving that a woman can be a serious swordsman.

Her later development should grow from what she believes people owe to weapons, history, and ownership.

## Failure modes to watch

Do not flatten her into "sword nerd Tashigi" with no moral stakes.

Do not make her so indifferent to prejudice that the surrounding world stops feeling real.

Do not make her weapon obsession automatically correct. It should be capable of becoming possessive or institutionally naive.
