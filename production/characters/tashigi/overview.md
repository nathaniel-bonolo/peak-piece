# Tashigi

## Core

Peak Tashigi is obsessed with swords and weapons.

Her identity is not built around proving that a woman can be a legitimate swordsman.

She already knows she is strong.

If somebody tries to reduce her to "a woman swordsman," she does not need to make their prejudice the center of her life. She may barely care enough to answer if there is a sword, weapon, or piece of craftsmanship in front of her that is more interesting.

This does not mean sexism does not exist in the world. It means Peak does not make defeating somebody else's sexism Tashigi's primary internal motivation.

## Weapon obsession

Tashigi cares about:

- sword grades and provenance
- craftsmanship
- famous blades
- stolen or misused weapons
- who carries a blade and why
- what it means to preserve, recover, or "save" a sword

That obsession can eventually become its own character problem.

Collecting a famous sword is not automatically the same thing as respecting the life, history, or current wielder around it.

## Return to Sabaody

Tashigi serves under Smoker at the Sabaody chokepoint.

She intercepts Zoro because he is a pirate carrying blades she wants seized and collected. Marine authority gives her a lawful reason to pursue an obsession she already has.

Seeing **Shusui** makes her even more determined to stop him.

Zoro does not need to defeat her unconscious.

Their clash is part of a fighting retreat. Zoro's job is to prevent Tashigi from reaching the Sunny until the crew is clear.

The encounter should still establish that both trained during the two years and that Zoro's growth is enormous.

When the escape window opens, Zoro leaves rather than turning the interception into a ceremonial duel.

## Long-term direction

Tashigi's later arc should grow from her relationship with weapons rather than from repeatedly asking whether she can compete with men.

Potential questions include:

- When does "saving" a sword become possession?
- Does a Government collection have a better moral claim to a blade than the person carrying it?
- Can a weapon have a history worth preserving without treating it as more important than people?

Exact later execution remains open.
