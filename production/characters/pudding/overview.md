# Charlotte Pudding

## Core

Pudding is a memory specialist whose Fruit forces her to relive memories in first person.

She does not simply watch a recording.

If somebody remembers being stabbed, Pudding remembers being that person and being stabbed.

If somebody remembers loving a daughter for twenty years, Pudding temporarily carries the emotional reality of that love.

## The important limit

**One person's memory ecology is usually manageable.**

The major identity danger begins when Pudding moves through **multiple people**, especially in a short period and especially when she repeatedly searches for the same emotionally intense category such as fear.

Foreign associations begin crossing into one another.

Pudding reaches for one person's fear and finds another person's childhood attached to it.

That is identity drift.

## Mundane residue

Drift is not only trauma.

Pudding can accumulate ordinary emotional associations that never belonged to her.

Examples:

- looking at a flower and suddenly feeling deeply fond of it
- meeting a stranger and wanting to hug them without knowing why
- seeing a ladybug and becoming afraid even though Pudding was never afraid of insects
- tasting food she normally dislikes and feeling homesick
- recognizing a place she has never personally visited
- remembering affection before remembering whose affection it was

Sometimes she can say:

> **That isn't mine.**

Sometimes:

> **...I think.**

## Journals

### Journal One: Who am I?

Pudding records her own life, preferences, habits, tiny memories, and identity anchors.

Her family also contributes memories **of Pudding**.

This matters because Pudding herself can become unreliable evidence about which experience belongs to her.

Family entries can be affectionate, embarrassing, stupid, irritating, or mundane.

They do not need to sound therapeutic.

The point is:

> other people remember Pudding from the outside

### Journal Two: Who have I carried?

Pudding records people whose memories remain with her.

The journal can preserve tiny things rather than only intelligence:

- he loved yellow flowers
- she feared ladybugs
- he had a daughter
- somebody I carried knew that woman; I do not remember who

The second journal prevents Pudding from treating every foreign feeling as contamination that must be erased.

## Pre-extraction ritual

Before a serious extraction, Pudding writes **one or two mundane sentences** at the top of the page.

The first thing is always her name.

Example:

> **Charlotte Pudding. I burned my tongue on tea this morning.**

That is enough.

No speech.

No checklist.

No elaborate ceremony.

It gives her one fresh piece of ordinary life that definitely belongs to her before she enters somebody else.

## Mature direction

Her early instinct may be:

> **These memories are not mine.**

Her mature understanding is closer to:

> **This feeling can be real without having started with me.**

And:

> **I decide what I carry forward.**
