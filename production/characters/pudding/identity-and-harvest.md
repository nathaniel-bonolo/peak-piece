# Pudding: Identity and Harvest

## Pre-extraction anchor

Before entering a serious memory extraction, Pudding writes something stupid or mundane.

The first thing is always her name.

Example:

> **Charlotte Pudding. I burned my tongue on tea this morning.**

One or two sentences are enough.

Do **not** expand this into a checklist, mantra, or elaborate ritual.

The point is a fresh mundane fact that unquestionably belongs to Pudding.

## Why multiple people are dangerous

One coherent life is usually navigable.

The major danger comes when Pudding carries several people's emotional associations at once.

Fear extraction is especially dangerous because she repeatedly enters the same kind of pathway.

Different lives begin cross-linking inside her.

That is when she can stop knowing why she:

- loves a flower
- fears a ladybug
- wants to hug a stranger
- misses a home she never lived in
- recognizes a voice
- feels parental love toward a child she never met

The feeling is real.

Its origin may not be hers.

## Journal One

Journal One is a collaborative answer to:

> **Who am I?**

Pudding contributes her own memories.

Family members contribute memories of her.

Entries can be:

- funny
- petty
- affectionate
- embarrassing
- mundane
- contradictory

Do not make every entry profound.

Do not overuse the backwards-salute gag as the example.

The journal matters because other people can remember Pudding when Pudding is temporarily bad at remembering where she ends.

## Journal Two

Journal Two asks:

> **Who have I carried?**

It records people and residue Pudding does not want to collapse into herself or erase.

The notes can be tiny.

> He loved yellow flowers.

> She was afraid of ladybugs.

> He had a daughter he promised to come home to.

The journal is partly respect for the fact that those emotions belonged to people before Pudding carried them.

## Locked pirate-crew scene direction

Pudding performs memory extraction across a captured pirate crew.

The first several are manageable.

By the final man, she is carrying too many people.

She goes in looking for fear.

She follows the network too far.

She experiences large pieces of his life.

He has a daughter.

He promised he would come home with a gift.

Immediately before Pudding enters, his recent thoughts include an apology to his daughter because he now believes he will never return.

Pudding sees the daughter's smile.

She feels the father's love.

For a period inside the memory, she does not merely observe:

> **he loves his daughter**

She experiences:

> **I love my daughter**

That distinction matters.

## Coming out

Pudding returns visibly confused.

The preparation staff needs actionable fear information.

She gives them something like:

> **"Needles. Spiders. ...daughter?"**

The first two are the requested answer.

"Daughter" reveals that Pudding went deeper than she intended.

The staff should not automatically use the daughter as the torture lever.

If that would destroy the man's coherence, it risks **bruising**.

They use enough of what Pudding found to produce fear and eventual surrender.

## The harvest

The man eventually submits to Linlin while still recognizably himself.

Linlin extracts him.

The resulting weapon settles rather than feeling as though it is constantly fighting her.

Pudding watches sadly because she has carried enough of the man's life that his death does not feel abstract.

This does not automatically make her renounce her family.

## The gift

Afterward Pudding finds / retrieves the gift he intended for his daughter.

She returns to her room.

She writes:

> **Your father spent his last thoughts thinking about you. He died.**

When she reaches the signature, she hesitates.

For a moment the father's relationship to the daughter is still too close inside her.

She eventually signs:

> **Charlotte Pudding**

That signature is a small act of orientation.

She then records in Journal Two:

> He had a daughter. He promised he would come back with a gift.

Exact wording can change in the finished scene.

## Linlin intercepts the package

Totto Land's ordinary outgoing-security process flags the package because:

- it originates from Pudding
- it is connected to a recently harvested pirate
- it is being sent to an outside civilian

The package is escalated.

Linlin checks the message for:

- military secrets
- locations
- harvest information
- dangerous operational details

There are none.

She says:

> **Send it.**

This is not remorse for harvesting the father.

She allows it because **Pudding needs to complete the promise she is carrying**.

## Linlin comes to Pudding

Afterward Linlin goes to Pudding's room.

She does not explain identity theory.

She tells Pudding stories about **Pudding**.

One locked memory:

Pudding was once so good at hiding during hide-and-seek that Katakuri dispatched three search parties.

Pudding starts to remember.

Linlin keeps talking.

Eventually Pudding falls asleep.

Linlin sleeps beside her.

## Night beat

Pudding wakes from a nightmare.

For several seconds she is disoriented and reaches automatically for Journal One.

Then she notices Mama beside her.

She smiles.

She lets go of the journal.

She goes back to sleep.

For one night, she does not need written proof.

Somebody beside her remembers who she is.

## Why the scene matters

Linlin can:

- turn the father into a toy
- feel no regret about the harvest
- let his final gift reach his daughter because Pudding needs it
- sit beside Pudding and lovingly reconstruct her childhood
- later continue running the same empire

None of those facts erase the others.

> **Contradiction is not inconsistency.**
