# Charlotte Katakuri

## Core

Katakuri is a roughly 200-year-old commander.

He does not have literal future sight.

His "prediction" comes from centuries of observing:

- posture
- weight
- breathing
- eyes
- muscles
- weapons
- formations
- emotions
- habits

He is extraordinarily good at predicting what people are about to do.

He can still be wrong, fooled, overloaded, or fatigued.

## Orchard role

Katakuri does **not** hunt high-bounty pirates for Government bounty money.

His relevant imperial job is much darker:

> **He is the Charlotte Empire's premier high-value harvester.**

A high bounty can cause Charlotte intelligence to evaluate somebody because it is a public signal that the person may have an unusually valuable soul.

The number itself is not trusted as a power level.

Katakuri is sent when the evaluation says a target is worth personally retrieving.

Typical reasons:

- the target is **Ripe**
- a high-potential target has dangerous **spoilage risk**
- the target must arrive alive
- ordinary retrieval forces are likely to damage or lose them
- the target is strong enough that precise capture matters

His observation and predictive skill make him unusually good at subduing dangerous people without accidentally destroying the soul Linlin wants.

If a target is **Green**, high-potential, and low-risk, the Charlotte system may deliberately let them continue growing.

See [[production/lore/factions/charlotte-orchard|The Orchard]].

## Bounty judgment

Katakuri understands that bounty and combat value are not identical.

A bounty may be inflated by politics or information.

A relatively unknown fighter may be underpriced.

The Charlotte Empire therefore uses bounty as a lead, not a verdict.

## Flaw

Katakuri spent two centuries becoming excellent at anticipating what people will do and accidentally started treating that as understanding them.

He may correctly predict that somebody intends to leave and open the door for them when what they actually need is for somebody to ask them to stay.

His growth is not becoming socially incompetent.

It is learning that observation cannot answer every human question.
