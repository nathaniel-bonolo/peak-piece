# Hordy's Letters

## Voice rule

Hordy rarely writes names.

His apologies usually begin:

> **To the...**

followed by what he did to the person.

That is how guilt organizes his memory.

Early letters contain a defense.

Later letters stop trying to acquit him.

He burns every letter.

There is no hidden archive that posthumously proves Hordy was good.

The audience sees the words.

Almost nobody in-world ever does.

The final letter is washed into unreadable pulp before Shirahoshi arrives.

## First letter

This follows the death of a young Fishman during a shift and Hordy ordering the body moved like broken equipment.

> **To the boy I told them to throw away,**
>
> I don't know your name.
>
> You stopped moving during the shift.
>
> The others wanted to carry you somewhere else.
>
> I told them to leave you until the bell.
>
> When the guard came, I told them to throw you with the others.
>
> I said it like you were broken equipment.
>
> They were watching me.
>
> If I stopped the work, they would have punished everyone.
>
> I know that.
>
> I know why I did it.
>
> But when they picked you up, your head fell backwards and I saw your face.
>
> You were younger than I thought.
>
> I'm sorry.
>
> I don't know what else I was supposed to do.
>
> I'm sorry.

This is the night the bathroom ritual begins.

## The east gate

> **To the woman I stopped at the east gate,**
>
> You almost made it.
>
> I saw you before the guard did.
>
> If I kept walking, maybe you would have reached the water.
>
> Maybe you would have escaped.
>
> Or maybe they would have found you tomorrow and punished the whole block.
>
> I grabbed you.
>
> You begged me quietly because you knew shouting would bring them.
>
> I told you to stop embarrassing yourself.
>
> I didn't mean that.
>
> I needed you to stop.
>
> They gave you twelve.
>
> It would have been thirty if the guard caught you.
>
> I know twelve is less than thirty.
>
> I keep telling myself that.
>
> I'm sorry.

## The ration

This letter is important because Hordy is no longer doing everything for harm reduction.

Sometimes he simply benefits.

> **To the man whose ration I took,**
>
> You said I eat better than you now.
>
> You were right.
>
> I told you rank has privileges.
>
> I sounded like them.
>
> I could have given it back after the guards left.
>
> I didn't.
>
> I was hungry.
>
> That's all.
>
> There isn't a better reason.
>
> I'm sorry.

## The four downstairs

Hordy has become good enough to suppress rebellion before it exists publicly.

> **To the four I locked downstairs,**
>
> You hadn't done anything yet.
>
> I heard what you were planning.
>
> I knew which guard you were going to attack.
>
> I knew where you hid the pipe.
>
> So I stopped you before anybody got hurt.
>
> They congratulated me.
>
> Production didn't stop for even an hour.
>
> Nobody else was punished.
>
> That should mean I did the right thing.
>
> One of you asked me how I knew.
>
> I didn't answer.
>
> I know because I know how you think now.
>
> I'm sorry.

## Devil's Hand

> **To the one who called me Devil's Hand,**
>
> They laughed when you said it.
>
> I hit you.
>
> I needed them to think the name didn't bother me.
>
> I hit you again.
>
> That one wasn't for them.
>
> I was angry.
>
> You were right and I hated you for saying it.
>
> I'm sorry.

The "but" is disappearing.

## The woman who thanked him

> **To the woman who thanked me,**
>
> Don't.
>
> Please don't.
>
> I changed twenty lashes to eight.
>
> You looked at me afterward and said thank you.
>
> I don't know why that hurt more than when people spit at me.
>
> You should not have to thank me for deciding how badly you're allowed to be beaten.
>
> I'm sorry.

## Drunk

By now Hordy sometimes invents noble explanations after ordinary cruelty.

> **To the man I hit too hard tonight,**
>
> There was no guard watching.
>
> You weren't going to start anything.
>
> You annoyed me.
>
> I was drunk.
>
> I hit you.
>
> I told myself afterward that you had been acting suspicious.
>
> That is a lie.
>
> I'm sorry.

## Shirahoshi

After helping place Shirahoshi in a cell:

> **To the girl I put in a cell,**
>
> You looked at me like I was afraid.
>
> I am.
>
> I've seen people do what you're doing.
>
> They stand up.
>
> Everybody gets excited.
>
> Everybody thinks this time will be different.
>
> Then the people with the whips come.
>
> Tiger made people believe strength would save them.
>
> My friends believed him.
>
> They put Gems in their blood.
>
> I watched them die.
>
> Now you're telling people to stand up again.
>
> You don't know what hope costs.
>
> I put you in that cell because I don't want to watch it happen again.
>
> That's what I tell myself.
>
> I'm sorry.

## Final letter

The final letter returns to the person Hordy whipped when Devil's Hand was born.

The opening is locked:

> **To the one I whipped when you pushed the guard,**

Current full direction:

> **To the one I whipped when you pushed the guard,**
>
> I still don't know your name.
>
> I remember your face.
>
> You pushed him.
>
> He fell.
>
> I saw the others reaching for their whips.
>
> I knew what would happen.
>
> So I took one first.
>
> I hit you.
>
> You looked at me like I was one of them.
>
> I hated you for that.
>
> For years I told myself you were wrong.
>
> I thought if I held the whip, I could make it hurt less.
>
> If somebody had to decide who suffered, I thought it should be me.
>
> Then they gave me more people.
>
> More rooms.
>
> More authority.
>
> More things I could decide.
>
> And every time I told myself the same thing.
>
> Better me than them.
>
> Better eight lashes than twenty.
>
> Better one prisoner than fifty.
>
> Better one dead Fishman than a whole block.
>
> Better me.
>
> Always better me.
>
> They used to tell us the Sea King would come.
>
> Then they told us Tiger would come.
>
> Nobody came.
>
> I thought that meant I had to become strong enough to come for us myself.
>
> Today the Sea King came.
>
> It didn't come for me.
>
> It came for her.
>
> It took my right hand.
>
> The hand that held the whip.
>
> I think I'm glad.
>
> I don't have to hold it anymore.
>
> You were right.
>
> I became one of them.
>
> I'm sorry.

There is no "but" after the final apology.

## Final staging

Hordy reaches for the old pen with a right hand that no longer exists.

He gets it down awkwardly.

The left-handed writing is bad.

He finishes.

He tries to burn the page.

The lighter slips onto the floor.

He looks at it.

Laughs.

Leaves it.

The shower destroys the only letter he ever failed to burn.

Nobody gets to read his confession.

History receives no correction.
