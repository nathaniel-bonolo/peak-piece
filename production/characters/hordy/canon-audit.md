# Hordy Jones: Canon Audit

## Canon baseline

Canon Hordy is deliberately revealed as someone whose hatred is not rooted in a personal atrocity committed against him.

The Fish-Man District environment transmits hatred to him.

## What canon appears to be doing

Canon Hordy embodies inherited hatred reproduced socially even when the inheritor did not personally experience the originating grievance.

Peak Piece understands that function.

## Peak judgment

We intentionally reject Hordy as the pure vehicle for that thesis.

"This is intentional in canon" is not enough reason to preserve it.

Peak prefers a Hordy whose choices can be followed through:

- survival
- harm reduction
- collaboration
- efficiency
- privilege
- guilt
- addiction
- control
- failed liberation

## Peak thesis

> **What happens when an oppressed person learns that becoming the oppressor's hand is the safest way to protect the oppressed?**

The answer is not:

> trauma makes him right

The answer is:

> a survival strategy can work just well enough to become an identity, and that identity can become oppressive long after the original excuse stops being true

## Why direct suffering does not absolve him

Hordy's backstory gives him real reasons to hate the systems that hurt him.

It also gives him agency.

He repeatedly makes choices.

Early choices are heavily constrained.

Later choices are less constrained.

He reduces some punishments, stops rebellions, improves production, accepts privileges, keeps benefits, sometimes hurts people for selfish reasons, knows when he is inventing excuses, and eventually seeks enough power to decide liberation on everyone else's behalf.

The audience can understand him without being asked to agree with him.

## Why the kapo role matters

Hordy's core contradiction only works if collaboration produces real short-term benefits.

If he never saves anyone from worse punishment, his rationalization is obviously fake from day one.

Peak lets the strategy work locally:

> twelve lashes really can be less than thirty

Then asks what happens when the person deciding between twelve and thirty starts believing that makes him morally entitled to hold the whip.

## Devil's Hand

The title is earned through action.

Hordy becomes valuable because he understands oppressed Fishmen better than their human overseers.

He can stop rebellion before guards know it exists.

That makes him both protector in some individual moments and an increasingly efficient part of the machine.

## Fisher Tiger

Hordy blaming Tiger is intentionally unfair.

His friends died attempting crude Gem injection because Tiger's legend convinced them impossible liberation might be possible.

Hordy turns that pain into:

> hope is dangerous

rather than:

> the mine forced desperate people into desperate choices

This is displacement, not authorial agreement with Hordy.

## Shirahoshi

Peak Shirahoshi becomes the person who most directly contradicts Hordy's survival philosophy.

She also admires Tiger.

So Hordy sees her as dangerous before he sees her as useful.

Their relationship is political, personal, and mechanical through Gem injection.

## The ending does not redeem him

The right hand is removed by a Sea King while Hordy is choking Shirahoshi.

He feels relief because he no longer has to be Devil's Hand.

That does not undo the whipping, suppression, people he hurt, system he improved, or violence he commits after gaining power.

His final confession is private.

Shirahoshi never reads it.

Fishman Island never discovers a secret archive proving Hordy was misunderstood.

History is allowed to remember the monster.

The audience is allowed to understand the person.

## What we preserve from canon

- Fishman anger
- social pressure
- radicalization
- the danger of hatred
- a villain whose politics threaten liberation
- the idea that environment can manufacture monsters

## What we intentionally reject

The "nothing happened personally to me" structure.

Peak is not attempting to preserve that exact Hordy thesis.

## Failure modes

Do not:

- make slavery automatically justify later violence
- secretly reveal Hordy was a saint
- make every punishment he gives a hidden act of mercy
- let the final letter erase responsibility
- let Shirahoshi publicly rehabilitate him
- turn Tiger into the actual cause of his friends' deaths
- make Hordy's guilt more important than the victims' suffering
