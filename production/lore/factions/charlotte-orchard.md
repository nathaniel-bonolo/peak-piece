# The Charlotte Orchard

## Core

Linlin treats unusually strong people as potential future soul material.

Strong souls are rare.

She therefore does **not** automatically harvest every dangerous pirate as soon as the Charlotte Empire can reach them.

Some are worth more later.

The Charlotte intelligence system calls its tracked pool of promising people:

> **The Orchard**

Linlin herself is less clinical.

She may simply call a tracked person:

> **one of mine**

or describe somebody as still being **on the vine**.

The exact dialogue can vary. Do not invent another formal vocabulary layer unless the story needs it.

## Bounties are the first screen, not a power level

The World Government already maintains a giant public list of dangerous / politically important people.

The Charlotte Empire uses bounties as a cheap first-pass signal:

> **This person may be worth evaluating.**

A bounty does **not** equal soul strength.

It can be distorted by:

- political threat
- knowledge
- civilian harm
- reputation
- inaccurate reporting
- institutional bias

Charlotte intelligence therefore evaluates the person behind the number.

## Evaluation axes

### Current value

How strong / coherent is the soul **now**?

Relevant evidence can include combat ability, Haki, Devil Fruit use, experience, will, age, injuries, and observed performance.

### Growth potential

How much stronger is the person plausibly going to become?

A rapidly rising young pirate may be much more valuable in several years.

### Spoilage risk

How likely is the prospect to become unavailable before reaching that potential?

Risk can include:

- death
- crippling injury
- reckless behavior
- imprisonment
- disappearance
- entering another Emperor's protection
- disease
- a war likely to kill them

A prospect with enormous upside but absurd spoilage risk can be harvested early.

## Locked vocabulary

### Green

Valuable, but still meaningfully below projected ceiling.

If potential is high and spoilage risk is low, leave them alive and track them.

### Ripe

Estimated close enough to their useful ceiling that waiting no longer justifies the risk.

Ready for harvest.

### Overripe

Declining, aging, damaged, or otherwise likely to lose value.

Harvest immediately if still worth taking.

### Spoilage risk

Probability that a tracked prospect dies, disappears, becomes inaccessible, or loses major value before maturity.

### Harvest

Capture, preparation, submission, and soul extraction.

### Bruising

Psychological damage severe enough to reduce the captive's coherence or create a worse / more resistant resulting weapon.

Bruising is not compassion.

It is damage to an expensive future soul-weapon.

### Toys

Linlin's personal word for completed soul-weapons.

She can have favorites.

She can remember who they came from without treating the dead person as somebody she regrets killing.

## Tracking

A Green prospect with high potential and low spoilage risk may be allowed to leave.

The Charlotte network tries to retain a future tracking route.

Current direction:

- obtain Vivre Card material if possible
- keep intelligence files current
- watch bounty changes
- monitor crew losses, injuries, wars, territory, and alliances

Do not require every tracked person to know they were marked.

A frightening reveal can be that somebody thought the Charlottes spared them when the real answer was:

> **They thought you would be worth more later.**

## Linlin's time horizon

Linlin is roughly four centuries old.

Waiting years for a promising pirate to mature is emotionally easy for her in a way it is not for ordinary people.

Her logic is simple:

> **do not harvest a promising soul before it matures**

Waiting can produce a stronger future weapon.

## Emperor friction

Linlin already thinks of Orchard prospects as hers.

If another Emperor kills one, recruits one permanently, cripples one, or otherwise destroys the future harvest, Linlin can take it personally.

Possible tone:

> **Kaido spoiled one of mine.**

The conflict is not a stock-price joke.

It is Linlin's possessiveness colliding with another Emperor's freedom to act.

## Katakuri's role

[[production/characters/katakuri/overview|Katakuri]] is the Charlotte Empire's premier high-value harvester.

He is especially useful when:

- the target is Ripe
- the target must arrive alive
- ordinary capture teams may accidentally destroy the asset
- escape would be costly
- a high-spoilage Green target has to be taken early

His observation and prediction make him unusually good at subduing dangerous people without ruining the harvest.

He does **not** hunt them to collect Government bounty money.

The bounty is merely one signal that gets a person evaluated.

## Pudding's role

[[production/characters/pudding/overview|Pudding]] helps prepare captured targets.

Her job is not "make them as scared as possible."

It is to identify:

- the fears most likely to produce extraction yield
- the emotional route most likely to make the captive surrender
- the point beyond which the process risks bruising them
