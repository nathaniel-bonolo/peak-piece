# Poneglyphs

> **Foundational direction:** the World Government cannot solve the Poneglyph problem by destroying the stones.

## Core problem

If the World Government genuinely wants the Void Century erased, surviving public monuments need a reason to exist.

Recharted's answer is structural:

> **The stones are effectively indestructible to the Government.**

Therefore the Government attacks the thing it **can** destroy:

> **the ability to read, teach, study, reach, and connect them.**

## Most stones are not permanently guarded

There are too many Poneglyphs to place an elite army beside every one forever.

Most can remain where history left them.

Government suppression focuses on:

- scholars
- language traditions
- archives
- teaching chains
- excavation / research
- transport information
- institutions capable of comparing multiple stones

This makes Ohara-style suppression part of the actual containment strategy rather than an oddly indirect side policy.

## High-danger stones

Especially dangerous stones can receive stronger measures:

- burial
- sealing
- military installations
- restricted islands
- proxy guardians
- treaty control
- access denial

Road Poneglyphs are obvious candidates for exceptional containment because combining them creates a navigational route rather than isolated historical fragments.

## The Government accidentally creates the pirate gauntlet

People notice that certain indestructible stones attract absurd levels of secrecy and force.

Rumors can grow organically:

> guarded stones → forbidden route → hidden truth / treasure → pirate obsession

The Government does **not** need to intentionally advertise a One Piece myth.

Its own containment behavior makes the stones look important.

The irony is that locking forbidden history into separate inaccessible pieces can help create the ultimate pirate challenge.

## Roger / Pirate King

Pirate King is not simply "the strongest pirate alive."

Roger becomes Pirate King because he uniquely completes the distributed gauntlet:

- survives the Grand Line
- gains access to readers / deciphering
- penetrates multiple political locks
- acquires the necessary Road Poneglyph information
- reaches the destination no other pirate reached

This preserves room for other monsters to be older, stronger in a matchup, richer, or more territorially powerful without automatically becoming Pirate King.

## God Valley

A Poneglyph can be one strategic reason the Government values God Valley.

It should **not** become the reason every independent character arrives there.

Native Hunting, slaves, prizes, Rocks objectives, Roger, Garp, personal relationships, and other motives should remain independent where they already work.

> **Use the Poneglyph to explain Government strategic value, not to compress every character's motive into one cause.**

## Open implementation questions

The foundation does **not** yet lock:

- exact material composition
- how difficult movement is
- why dumping every movable stone into ocean/magma is not a complete solution
- which exact stones the Government knows about
- exact Road Poneglyph custody history

Those questions need answers when the story reaches them.

Do not silently invent universal rules only to close a spreadsheet early.
