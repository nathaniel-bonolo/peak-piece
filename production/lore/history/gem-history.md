# Gem History

## Ancient deposits

Natural Gem deposits once existed across a much larger accessible world.

## The flood as strategic denial

Roughly 800 years ago, the sea level rose dramatically.

Many major Gem deposits became submerged.

Current Peak direction:

Imu / the emerging World Government had already accumulated large reserves and control over important extraction/refining infrastructure.

Flooding therefore does more than destroy territory.

It can:

- bury rival deposits
- destroy competing infrastructure
- turn surface-accessible energy into underwater energy
- make old stockpiles disproportionately valuable
- leave the faction that prepared beforehand materially dominant

This does **not** have to be the only motive for the flood.

But it gives the catastrophe geopolitical depth beyond destruction for destruction's sake.

## Fishmen after the flood

Fishmen become far more strategically important because they can access energy deposits surface powers cannot reach easily.

That creates both leverage and vulnerability.

## World Government dominance

The Government's advantage can rest on:

- ancient stockpiles
- refining
- shipping
- legal control
- military power
- information suppression
- coerced extraction
- control of trade routes

A challenger may possess powerful individuals and still struggle to compete with a state that has centuries of strategic reserves.

## Haki concentration

Basic Haki does not require Gems.

However, Gem access helps explain why the most spectacular sustained high-output Haki applications are concentrated among elite factions with strategic reserves.

This works alongside [[production/lore/history/haki-history|Haki knowledge suppression]], not instead of it.

## Whitebeard disruption

Whitebeard's protection of Fishmen attacks more than slavery as an isolated moral crime.

By freeing workers and helping Fishman Island retain bargaining power, he interferes with one of the world's most important extraction relationships.

That can tighten supply, raise prices, and reduce Government leverage.
