# Backwards Salute Batch

## Setup

Pudding has performed Biscuit Reserve memory-template work so many times that it has become routine.

Mama finishes the soul stage.

Someone reminds Pudding that the new batch still needs its memory package.

Pudding says she will do it later.

She stays up too late, drinks too much at some family event, gets distracted, overworks herself, or whatever exact mundane reason fits the chapter.

Then at an awful hour she remembers:

> oh shit, the biscuits

She has done the checklist a thousand times.

So she rushes it.

That is the mistake.

Not stupidity.

Routine.

## Test

The new Biscuit Reserve batch forms.

Cracker runs the drills.

> March.

Perfect.

> Turn.

Perfect.

> Formation.

Perfect.

> Salute.

Every biscuit soldier salutes backwards.

Silence.

Cracker slowly looks at Pudding.

Pudding looks at the soldiers.

Looks at Cracker.

> ...shit.

Exact dialogue remains open.

## Why they keep it

The defect is harmless.

The soldiers:

- fight correctly
- follow orders
- hold formation
- identify targets
- perform every meaningful battlefield behavior

They just salute backwards.

Pudding can rebuild / reinstall the template.

Cracker asks what that costs.

She tells him.

The answer is expensive enough that Cracker effectively decides:

> **They salute backwards now.**

No heroic fix.

No emergency patch.

The empire lives with the inconvenience.

## Why this matters

This is a model Peak Piece joke:

> **character + circumstance + existing system**

Nobody becomes stupid for the punchline.

The system does not become less rigorous.

The rigor creates the specific way the mistake can happen.

It also demonstrates:

> **Competent systems are not perfect systems.**

## Continuity

That particular batch should continue saluting backwards in later appearances.

Charlotte characters do not need to explain it every time.

It is normal to them now.

## Possible later Pudding payoff

Do **not** write this gag only as setup.

It is allowed to remain a joke forever.

But if Pudding later struggles to trust her own memories, the backwards-saluting batch can become an accidental external fingerprint.

She sees them.

Somebody beside her says:

> You did that.

And for a moment she knows:

> ...yeah. I did.

No giant speech required.

If that later beat never fits naturally, do not force it.
