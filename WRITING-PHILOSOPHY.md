# Writing Philosophy

## Audit everything

A cool premise is not enough.

Ask what it implies, what it costs, who notices, who exploits it, who pays for it, how institutions respond, and what changes after it is used.

The goal is not realism for realism's sake. The goal is causality.

## Do the homework so the story can feel effortless

The internal project can be much more rigorous than the reader-facing story.

We can maintain:

- spreadsheets
- tactical doctrine
- economic assumptions
- resource budgets
- timelines
- logistics
- information-state notes
- second- and third-order consequences

The story does **not** need to stop and explain all of that.

The reader should mostly experience the result:

- a route becomes unsafe
- an army changes doctrine
- a resource runs low
- a commander chooses between two costs
- a character adapts because they learned something

Deep structure should create natural storytelling, not turn the chapter into a technical manual.

## Let action do the talking

Do not explain what the audience can already infer.

A reaction should often synchronize the audience with the character rather than translate the scene into dialogue.

If a small action can carry the meaning, use the small action.

## Backstory explains. It does not manufacture value.

The audience should understand who a character is before the backstory arrives.

A backstory should recontextualize present behavior, not be required to make the character worth caring about.

## Earn emotion through accumulated meaning

The deeper the setup, the smaller the payoff is allowed to be.

A hand reaching for something that is no longer there can hit harder than a page of crying if the audience understands what that action means.

## Competent characters create better problems

Never make one side stupid merely to give the other side aura.

A character can make the correct decision and still lose because:

- the opponent had better preparation
- the opponent had information they did not
- the terrain favored the opponent
- the objective created a tradeoff
- the opponent anticipated the correct response

Both sides can gain credibility from the same exchange.

## Optimize for the character, not a chess engine

Peak Piece does **not** mean every character uses every ability in the mathematically optimal way.

Ask what the strongest use is that **this person** could plausibly discover, choose, and execute.

Their usable potential is constrained by:

- **Capability** — what they could theoretically do
- **Knowledge** — what they know is possible
- **Intelligence** — what connections they can make
- **Temperament** — what kinds of solutions occur naturally to them
- **Motivation** — how much effort they care to spend
- **Values** — what effective options they would refuse
- **Experience** — what habits and techniques they have actually built
- **Information** — what they know about the current situation
- **Pressure** — how much time, attention, and emotional control they have right now

A lazy genius can have enormous theoretical potential and still choose elegant low-effort solutions.

An intuitive fighter can discover applications a formal engineer would never think of.

A scientist should exploit mechanics differently from a brawler.

Character is part of the optimization problem.

## New situation, not new power

Prefer second- and third-order consequences of established mechanics over surprise ability inflation.

The best reveal should feel surprising in the moment and inevitable in retrospect.

## Strength does not determine fights by itself

Combat includes:

- position
- information
- logistics
- objectives
- preparation
- terrain
- timing
- attention
- morale
- resource budgets
- matchup
- ability to convert an advantage

Power matters. It is not the entire operating system of a fight.

## Systems should produce worldbuilding

If a power or technology is important, ask:

- Who pays?
- Who maintains it?
- What does it replace?
- Why use it instead of something cheaper?
- What happens when it fails?
- What institutions form around it?
- What black markets form around it?
- What happens after the miracle?

## The world learns

Arcs should become information.

Armies change doctrine. Governments adapt. Pirates learn counters. Technology spreads. Characters remember previous battles.

The world does not reset because the next arc started.

## Female characters

Write women by the same standard as men.

Give them goals, competence, flaws, relationships, agency, skills, failure, ambition, stupidity, brilliance, and consequences because they are people.

Gender can matter when the story has a reason for it to matter. It should not replace personality, goals, skill, or agency.

Do not reduce women to fanservice, romantic function, or a gendered writing gimmick.

Do not sexualise children or child-coded characters.

A useful test is:

> **If the character's gender changed, would their actual character still exist?**

## Preserve the fun

Peak Piece is still supposed to be weird, funny, adventurous, excessive, and imaginative.

The rewrite is not trying to sand off the absurdity.

The point is to make the absurdity matter.

Systems exist to create better adventures, problems, jokes, reveals, fights, and emotional payoffs, not to replace them.
